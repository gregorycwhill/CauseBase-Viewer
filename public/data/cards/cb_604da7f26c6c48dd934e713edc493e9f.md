## CauseBase summary

Environmental Justice Australia (EJA) is described on its website as a public-interest legal organisation based in Victoria that says it uses the law to address environmental and climate issues across the country. The website lists activities the organisation says it runs, including landmark court cases, high‑stakes legal interventions, legal advice and representation, and advocacy campaigns; named items on the site include cases titled "Hard Truths: Australia’s coal and gas exports case", a court case for threatened wildlife, the Great Northern Savanna case and the North West Shelf Extension case. EJA's website states it provides pro‑bono legal support to communities and to Traditional Owners to protect Country, nature and climate, and it describes standing "shoulder‑to‑shoulder with communities". The site also publishes webinars and legal updates and invites people to subscribe for updates. Regulatory filings for the 2022–23 year show revenue of $7,770,242 and total expenses of $5,338,457 for the reported period. The available evidence is primarily the organisation's own website text and an ACNC extract and does not include independent evaluations or detailed operational metrics.
