## CauseBase summary

Global Wildlife Fund Limited is described on its website as an Australian charity, based in New South Wales, that partners with local conservation organisations to fund and oversee overseas conservation projects. The organisation lists initial geographic priorities as Sub‑Saharan Africa, the Andes–Amazon, New Guinea and the Pacific, and Asian biodiversity hotspots. Website material and news items cited include wildlife translocations (for example, reintroduction of black rhino to Matusadona National Park and translocation of sable antelope in Zimbabwe), a Papua Community Forest Project that aims to help communities secure customary title and implement forest management to prevent industrial logging, and purchase of 250 hectares to expand Narupa Reserve in the Ecuadorian Amazon. The site names target species and groups such as black rhino, snow leopards, African wild dogs, hummingbirds, macaws, birds of paradise and rediscovered marsupials in Papua. The organisation states it negotiates project funding agreements, tracks project progress and reports back on outcomes and metrics. The website also states that directors and founders donate their time to research, assess, support and audit projects and that Australians can make tax‑deductible gifts.
